## v2: redesign

`AURORA Dashboard v2.dc.html`, October 24, 2026.

The redesign, built to the rules in DESIGN.md. It has three pages:
- **Caseload:** by next visit (the default), with a header for each day, or grouped by status. Safety-flagged patients come first in both.
- **Patient page:** patient detail and session prep merged into one page, which prints as the one-page session brief.
- **Patient handout:** a plain-language page for the patient.

What changed from v1:
- Safety flags follow the October 19 safety design: no crisis banner or Respond buttons.
- HAM-D is removed, pending `aurora-bxi`.
- MRN, diagnosis code and medication list are removed, because AURORA won't have EHR data.
- The text describes what changed rather than prescribing actions.
- The mock data has 10 patients, with visits spread over two weeks.
- Muted line icons mark the kind of signal (medication, sleep, check-ins, and so on). The icons are from [Lucide](https://lucide.dev), under the ISC license.
- **Distinct** sections add a white summary band on the patient page, small graphics in the severity and check-in tiles, a tinted Safety tile when there is a flag, "What changed" beside the goals, a shaded zone on the chart above baseline + 5, and tinted status bands on the caseload. Distinct was settled on October 25, and the one-column **Plain** style is no longer offered.
- **Larger** text follows the type rules in DESIGN.md: 17 px body text, nothing below 15 px, safety wording at body size, and print sizes of at least 9 pt (the handout prints at 14 pt). It is settled, and the earlier, smaller scale is no longer offered.
- On the patients list, resting on a row for a moment shows a peek card: a small trend, severity with its uncertainty and baseline, the latest PHQ-9, item 9, and what changed. Rows are reachable by Tab, and Enter opens the patient.
- The ⓘ icons open their explanation at once, when pointed at or reached by Tab, instead of the browser's delayed tooltip. A click or tap keeps it open; Escape or a click elsewhere closes it.
- On the patient page, pointing at the trend chart shows each day's values: the AI estimate and range, whether that day's check-in was done and what it estimated, the PHQ-9 and item 9 on PHQ-9 days, and any event. From the keyboard, Tab to the chart and use the arrow keys (Shift for a week at a time). In the mock data, check-ins are expected Monday to Saturday, so 12 in 14 days. The last 14 days match each patient's check-in dots, and earlier days follow the same rate.
- The trend chart draws each check-in's own AI estimate as a faint dot around the smoothed line, with the day-to-day scatter the detection-latency simulation assumes. The status rules read the line.
- Times are 12-hour by default ("2:00 PM"). Clinicians can switch to 24-hour in the account menu under their name, at top right. This setting is part of the design, not a review control.

## Landing page

`index.html` links to all three versions.

- **Headline:** it asks a question in the spirit of motivational interviewing ("What would you want to know before each session?"). The introduction offers the mockups as our answer so far and asks what we've missed.
- **Banner** (since October 25): the page opens with a slowly moving aurora banner. Moving a mouse over it makes nearby stars sparkle and eases the aurora slightly toward the pointer, for depth.
- **Links** (since October 25): each version's heading is a link, and every version's review strip has an "All versions" link back to the landing page.
- **Notes:** a Leave notes step explains that shared notes need the private review link, and offers a dialog to enter its access code. The mockups' Notes panel has the same dialog. Notes can be left on the landing page itself as well as in the mockups.
- **Tagline** (October 27): pointing at the AURORA mark shows "Rich context, shared understanding", as in v3.
- **Order:** How to review comes straight after v3. The earlier versions sit below it, oldest first, in a quieter "How we got here" section.
- **Plane lights** (November 1): the far-off airliner's red beacon now alternates with a white strobe, as an airliner's lights look from the ground. Each flashes every 2.4 s, so the plane still blinks only every 1.2 s.
- **A livelier sky** (November 1), still calm. With the reduced-motion setting nothing moves, but the time of day and the moon still show.
  - the sky follows the viewer's clock: a blue glow over the horizon at dusk, a paler one before dawn, a brighter, greener aurora around midnight, and late at night the cabin's fire burning low
  - tonight's moon, in its real phase
  - now and then an aurora surge, as in a substorm, for about 14 seconds
  - now and then a satellite, crossing behind the curtains and fading into the earth's shadow
  - the Big Dipper and the North Star, whose lines and names show when the pointer comes near
  - taps: the stars nearest a tap sparkle, and a tap on the Big Dipper shows it
  - clicking the cabin stokes its fire, with a puff of smoke and a few sparks; late at night it burns bright again for half a minute
- **Slower plane** (November 2): the far-off airliner takes about 2¼ to 2½ minutes to cross rather than 45 to 50 s, and comes about every 5 minutes rather than every 100 s or so, so it is in the sky about as much as before.
- **A real moon** (November 2): the moon has its real face, from NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter maps, with the maria, the craters and the rays around Tycho. It is lit for the date, so the terrain catches the light along the terminator, and beside a thin crescent the maria show faintly in the earthshine. Its phase now follows the moon's actual motion. It is nearly four times as wide as before, a prominent part of the sky, and drawn above the aurora, so it stays bright. It is pale gold with warm gray maria, the color of a moon this low in the sky. Stars stay at least a moon's width from its edge. On a phone it sits nearer the right edge, clear of the Big Dipper. The footer credits NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio.
- **Resting out of view** (November 2): the banner's animations pause while it is scrolled out of view, which takes its CPU use from about 2% of one core to about 0.3%.
- **No sideways scrolling** (November 2): on windows narrower than about 900 px, the glow behind the v3 screenshot no longer makes the page scroll sideways.
- **Light the moon** (November 2): the pointer acts as the sun, so the moon lights from wherever it is, from full over its middle to a crescent farther out, with shadows moving across the real terrain. A label gives tonight's phase, how much is lit and the next full moon. Clicking or tapping the moon plays a month of phases in five seconds.
- **Catch the shooting stars** (November 2): clicking or tapping the sky starts a small game in the spirit of Missile Command. Each click sends a spark up from the cabin's chimney that blooms where you clicked, and shooting stars that touch a bloom burst for points. A star's color follows its speed, and faster, smaller stars score more. A caught star stays in the sky, twinkling, for the rest of the game. Stars come gradually faster and more often until five have landed, each with a flash behind the trees, debris and smoke, and the browser saves scores and compares each game with the others. At the end, the caught stars join into a named constellation, the aurora surges and credits rise from behind the trees. Everyone with the review link also shares an arcade-style table of the ten best scores, with initials. With the reduced-motion setting there is no game.
- **More to the shooting star game** (November 5):
  - **Chain reactions:** a burst throws off a smaller bloom that can catch stars nearby, with the multiplier climbing along the chain, and some stars now fall in flurries.
  - **Real meteor showers:** waves are named for real showers and fly at their speeds, slowest first, fanning out from one point in the sky. On a shower's peak night, it opens the game for double points.
  - **Save the cabin:** the window and hearth dim with each life lost, and the last one puts the fire out until the game is over. A star landing right on the cabin costs two lives.
  - **Visitors:** a satellite is worth 500 points, hitting an airliner costs 150 ("Air traffic control has questions"), and a golden comet surges the aurora and bursts every star in the sky.
  - **Sound:** retro tones, off by default, with a speaker switch at the bottom right during a game. Catches rise in pitch along a chain, and a landing rumbles.
- **A near miss** (November 5): in the shooting star game, a bloom that touches the airliner now makes it duck, bank and climb away out of the sky, rather than fly on as if nothing had happened. It still costs 150 points.
- **A secret** (November 5): there's now a way to see the grand finale without bringing all three animals home. Arcade players may guess it, and the North Star knows another.

- **Smooth flight** (November 5): an airliner that gets across the sky without a bloom touching it is worth 100 points, and the credits count them.
- **A grand finale** (November 5): the first time all three animals have been brought home, fireworks rise from behind the forest and burst in their colors, building to a crescendo of gold, then the Big Dipper, the fox's face and Cancer blaze together, the aurora surges and credits say all three are home.
